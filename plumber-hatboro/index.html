Hatboro's housing stock: a median home built in 1950

The borough's Hatboro 2040 plan, using Montgomery County assessment data, puts the median Hatboro home at 1,479 square feet and built in 1950. Cape Cods make up the largest share, more than 600 homes or 27 percent. In a house that small, one tired drain or supply line touches most of the plumbing.

The plan dates York Gardens in the northwest corner and Mitchell Park east of York Road to the 1940s, Hatboro Heights to the 1930s and 1940s, and Hatboro Farms in the southwest corner mostly to the 1960s. The oldest buildings sit along York Road in the southern half, with the Victorian District around Byberry, Moreland and Montgomery Avenues in the center.

Your block's era tells you what to check first. Houses from the 1930s and 1940s often still carry galvanized steel supply pipe and clay or cast iron drains, while 1960s houses are more likely to have copper.

Sump pumps and backups lead the calls near Pennypack Creek

Hatboro is a hotspot on the county's map of structures in the 100-year floodplain, and the borough's plan says roads and private property flood in large rains.

The 1938 flood put the center of town under two feet of water, and Ida's remnants on September 1, 2021 brought more than 20 rescues, per the mayor's report to council. If your basement sits near the creek or a tributary, a working pit and pump is your first defense, which is why sump pump repair leads our Hatboro calls.

Test the pump before the storm. Pour a bucket of water into the pit, watch the float rise and the pump start, and confirm the discharge hose carries water away from the foundation. Storms that fill a pit often cut the power too, so ask about a battery backup. If water keeps coming in after the pump works, basement waterproofing covers the plumbing side of the fix.

Sewer backups come second. Hatboro's sewers run by gravity with no borough pump stations, so a drain that backs up in heavy rain is either a blocked lateral or a loaded main. Knowing which comes first.

Who owns the sewer lateral in Hatboro

Call the sewer authority first, then a plumber

Hatboro's sewers belong to the Upper Moreland-Hatboro Joint Sewer Authority (UMHJSA). Its policy says the owner owns the lateral from the house to the curb line and must clear the whole lateral. The Authority owns its collection line in the street and clears it at no cost to you if that line is the blockage.

The Authority asks you to call it first, not a plumber. Its sewer emergency line is 215-659-4017 and its after-hours line is 215-659-1462. We do not work on the Authority's main. If it reports its line is clear, we can run a camera inspection and sewer line cleaning on your lateral.

Burst pipe or active leak in Hatboro: what to do tonight

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or an active leak

These steps fit a small Hatboro house with a basement. Do them while you call.

Shut off the main

In many older Hatboro houses the main shutoff sits on the basement wall where the water line enters. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If it will not turn, do not force it, and tell us when you call.

Cut the power if it is dry to reach

If water is near the panel or the water heater, switch off that breaker only when you can reach it without stepping in water. If you cannot, stay out of the room and tell us.

Protect the area

Move boxes and electronics off the basement floor. If the water is dirty or smells like sewage, keep people and pets out and do not flush or run the washer.

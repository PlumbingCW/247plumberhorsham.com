Cost of Installing a Gas Tankless Water Heater in Horsham, PA

A gas tankless water heater installation in Horsham, PA is priced by the work around the unit more than by the unit itself. Gas line capacity, venting, a condensate drain, and the plumbing changes at the old tank location move the total further than the brand on the box.

This guide walks through each cost driver in the order a plumber checks them, so you can compare quotes on the same terms. We do not print flat prices here, because the real number depends on what is behind your walls.

Why the unit is the smaller part of the bill

A tank-type heater is forgiving. Its burner is often rated around 30,000 to 50,000 BTU per hour, it vents through a simple flue, and it sits on a pan near a drain. A whole-house gas tankless unit commonly asks for 150,000 to 199,000 BTU per hour at full fire, so your house has to deliver several times more gas at once.

That gap explains why two quotes for the same model can land far apart. One contractor may have priced a new gas line and a vent run, while the other assumed the old ones would do. Ask every bidder to list what is included before you compare totals.

What affects the price of a gas tankless install

These five items account for most of the difference between a straightforward swap and a larger project.

Gas piping and meter capacity

Pipe size, the distance from the meter, and the other gas appliances sharing the line decide whether the existing pipe can feed the new burner. A plumber sizes it against the unit's input rating using the fuel gas standard (NFPA 54 is the national reference). If the line is too small, upsizing it is often the largest single add.

Venting route

Condensing units usually vent through plastic pipe out a sidewall, while non-condensing units need stainless steel vent pipe. Run length, elbow count, and whether the vent exits through a wall or the roof all change labor and material. The manufacturer's vent table sets the limit, and a run that is too long makes the unit fault.

Condensate drain and neutralizer

Condensing models produce acidic condensate that needs a path to a drain, usually through a small neutralizer cartridge. A floor drain nearby keeps this simple. A utility room with no drain may need a condensate pump.

Water-side fittings

Isolation valves with flush ports let a plumber descale the heat exchanger later. Old galvanized pipe or crowded copper at the tank location may need replacing so the new connections are accessible and sound.

Location and power

The unit needs a 120-volt outlet for its fan and controls. Moving it to an exterior wall shortens the vent but lengthens the hot and cold runs, so relocating trades one cost for another.

Sizing: flow rate and temperature rise

A tankless heater is rated by how many gallons per minute it can heat by a given temperature rise. Incoming water in a Pennsylvania winter is much colder than in August, so a unit that keeps up in summer can fall short in January. Sizing for winter is the safer rule.

Swapping in place versus relocating the heater

Replace in the same spot

  • Reuses the existing gas stub and water lines when they pass inspection
  • Vent may still need new pipe since tank flues differ
  • Usually the lower-cost path
  • Condensate drain may still be missing

Move to a new wall

  • Shorter vent run to the outdoors
  • Longer hot and cold runs plus a new gas branch
  • Frees floor space in a basement or closet
  • More wall and ceiling opening, so more finish work

What the install visit covers

  1. 1

    Check gas supply and shutoffs

    The plumber confirms pipe size, tests for leaks, and shuts off gas and water at the right points before touching the old tank.

  2. 2

    Drain and remove the tank

    The old heater is drained, disconnected, and carried out. Sediment inside the tank is a quick clue about your water.

  3. 3

    Mount, vent, and connect

    The new unit goes on the wall, the vent and condensate line are run per the manufacturer's instructions, and gas and water are connected.

  4. 4

    Fire it and test it

    The plumber checks combustion operation, flow at a few fixtures, the temperature setting, and any error codes before explaining how to flush the unit yearly.

Running costs after the install

Many manufacturers recommend flushing a tankless unit about once a year, especially where water carries scale. Skipping that flush can shorten the life of the heat exchanger. Ask if your install includes isolation valves so the flush is a short job rather than a plumbing project.

Questions to ask before you accept any quote

  • Is the gas line size checked against the unit's BTU rating, and who pays if it is too small?
  • Is the vent type condensing plastic or stainless steel, and how long is the run?
  • Where does the condensate drain, and is a neutralizer included?
  • Are isolation valves with flush ports included?
  • Who pulls the permit if one is required, and who schedules the inspection?

Tankless heaters, Horsham hardness and the fuel in your house

Horsham Water & Sewer Authority publishes its source water hardness as ranging from about 200 to 350 mg/l as calcium carbonate, and suggests roughly 275 mg/l (16 grains per gallon) when a softener asks for incoming hardness. That is hard water. For a tankless buyer it means scale will coat the heat exchanger, so insist on isolation valves with flush ports and plan a yearly flush instead of waiting for error codes.

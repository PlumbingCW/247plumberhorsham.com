DIY Clogged Toilet Bowl Fixes for Horsham, PA Homes

A toilet bowl filling toward the rim is a bad moment, and what you do in the first minute decides how much ends up on the floor. Most single-toilet clogs in Horsham, PA homes can be cleared with one good plunger and a little patience.

This guide covers how to stop the rise, the order of fixes that actually work, what to avoid, and the signs that the clog is not in the toilet at all.

The first minute: stop the rise

Do not flush again. A second flush sends more water into a bowl that cannot drain, and the overflow lands on your floor. Take the tank lid off and push the rubber flapper down so no more water enters the bowl.

If the water keeps climbing, close the supply valve. It is the small oval or round handle on the wall or floor behind the toilet, and you turn it clockwise until it stops. Put a towel on the floor and a bucket nearby while you work.

Pick the right plunger or the job fights back

A cup plunger with a flat rim is made for sinks and flat surfaces. A toilet needs a flange plunger, the kind with a soft rubber sleeve that folds out below the cup. That sleeve fits into the drain opening and seals it so the push goes into the line and not back up into the bowl.

  1. 1

    Add water if the bowl is low

    The cup must be covered with water to push properly. Pour in a bucket from the tub tap if needed, but stop before it reaches the rim.

  2. 2

    Seal the plunger and start gently

    Fit the flange into the drain hole and push down slowly once to expel the air. A hard first push splashes dirty water out of the bowl.

  3. 3

    Pump steadily for 15 to 20 strokes

    Keep the seal and push and pull with a firm rhythm. The pull stroke is what dislodges many clogs, so do not stop early.

  4. 4

    Break the seal and watch the bowl

    If the water drops and swirls, pour a bucket of water in to test the flow. If nothing moves, repeat once or twice, then move to the next tool.

Dish soap and hot water, with limits

A good squirt of dish soap into the bowl followed by a bucket of hot tap water can lubricate a soft paper clog and help it slide through. Let it sit for 15 minutes before plunging again. The water should be hot from the tap, never boiling, because a pot of boiling water can crack a cold porcelain bowl.

This works best on paper and soft waste. It will not move a toy, a wipe wad or a hard object, and repeating it more than twice just fills the bowl with soapy water.

Skip the bottled drain opener

Chemical toilet openers sit in standing water and can burn skin if the bowl splashes. They also leave a hazardous soup in the bowl that a plumber must then work through.

They are especially a poor idea for homes with a septic tank, where they can harm the bacteria that keep the system working.

When the plunger fails: the toilet auger

A toilet auger, also called a closet auger, is a short cable inside a rigid tube with a curved tip made to travel through the toilet's trapway without scratching the porcelain. It reaches a few feet past the bowl and is sold at most hardware stores.

  1. 1

    Position the tip in the drain

    Pull the cable back, set the curved end into the drain hole, and keep the tube against the bowl.

  2. 2

    Crank slowly and push gently

    Turn the handle clockwise while feeding the cable in. Stop if you meet hard resistance, because it may be an object, not paper.

  3. 3

    Hook or break the clog

    Once the cable moves freely or the water drops, pull it out slowly. Wipe it with a rag as it comes out.

  4. 4

    Test with a bucket

    Flush once with the tank only. If the bowl empties quickly and refills, the clog is gone.

What clogs toilets, and what to stop flushing

Wipes labeled flushable

They hold together far longer than paper and snag on rough pipe walls. Throw them in the trash.

Too much paper at once

Low-flow toilets rely on a good flush. Heavy loads clog them. Flush twice if you need to.

Feminine products and cotton swabs

They do not break down and collect at the first bend.

Small objects

Toys, brushes and phone cases end up in the trapway. They need a pull, not a push.

Signs the problem is not the toilet

Run the bathroom sink and tub. If water gurgles up into the shower drain or the toilet bubbles when you drain the washing machine, the blockage is in a shared line. Plunging will not fix a line problem, and it may push dirty water out of a lower fixture.

Call a professional if

  • Two or more fixtures are slow or backing up at once
  • The toilet clogs again within a day or two of clearing it
  • The auger meets a hard stop that will not clear
  • Water or sewage appears in a floor drain or basement
  • You dropped something solid into the bowl
  • The toilet rocks, leaks at the base or runs constantly after the clog

A single-toilet clog that returns often may point to a partial blockage past the toilet or a toilet that was never set right. Our toilet repair and installation team can pull the toilet, inspect the flange and trapway, and reset it with a new wax ring. When several fixtures are backing up, sewer line cleaning is the right service, not more plunging.

Cleanup and safety after the clog

Wear rubber gloves and splash goggles while you work. Wash the plunger and auger with hot soapy water or a diluted bleach solution, never mixing bleach with any other cleaner. Disinfect the floor and anything touched by bowl water, and let it dry fully.

If the overflow soaked a wall or carpet, pull up what you can and run a fan. Our teams serve Ambler and Warrington as well as Horsham, and we can help when water has reached a lower floor.

Plumbing in Horsham: who owns which part of the sewer

Under the Horsham Water and Sewer Authority's rules, the Authority owns the service lateral out to the right-of-way or easement line, and the owner owns the building lateral from there to the house. The rules call for a cleanout at the right-of-way, at every bend and every 100 feet. Locate your cleanout before a plumber arrives, because that is where a blockage on your side gets reached.

The Authority asks to be called first on a sewer backup, and it checks the street main free on a blockage call. If the whole house is backing up, call (215) 672-8011. It also says most homeowner policies exclude sewer backups, so read your policy before an overflow happens.

The Authority's wipes and grease sheet says disposable wipes clog sewer pipes. A wipe that clears your toilet can still collect in the building lateral, which is yours to clear, so the trash can is the cheapest fix.

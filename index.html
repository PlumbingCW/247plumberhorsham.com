Emergency plumbing in Horsham Township

Horsham Township sits in Montgomery County, and its housing runs from older homes near the road corridors to newer developments and large office parks. That mix matters for plumbing. Older supply lines and cast iron drains fail in different ways than the PEX and PVC in newer houses, so the first question the plumber asks is when the house was built.

If you are not sure what to do first, start with the water. Turn off the main shutoff, usually near where the line enters the house, then open a faucet at the lowest level to drain the pipes. Call us while you do it, and we will stay on the line if you need help finding the valve.

The calls we take most

Clogged drains and sewer backups

A single slow sink is usually a branch clog. Several fixtures backing up together points at the main line, which needs a different tool. See drain cleaning and sewer line cleaning.

Burst and leaking pipes

Frozen pipes split in winter, and old fittings fail any month. Stop the water first, then read about burst pipe repair or leak detection for hidden leaks.

No hot water or a leaking tank

A puddle under the tank means water heater replacement is likely. Cold showers with no leak often come down to a water heater repair.

Basement water

A failed pump during a storm turns a damp basement into a flooded one. A sump pump repair the same day can save the floor and everything stored on it.

Call, then do these three things

While you wait for the plumber

  • Shut off the main water valve, or the fixture valve if only one is leaking.
  • Turn off the water heater power or gas if the tank is leaking or the pipes are empty.
  • Move valuables and electronics away from water, and stay clear of wet outlets.
  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before you call us.

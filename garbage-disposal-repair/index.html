A leaking disposal is the one that cannot wait. Water running down the housing pools in the cabinet and soaks the particleboard floor. Switch the unit off at the wall, put a bucket under it and call.

Jammed or humming: safe things you can try

Turn the power off at the switch, then at the breaker if you can reach it. Most disposals have a hex socket at the center of the underside that takes a 1/4 inch Allen wrench. Working the wrench back and forth frees many jams without opening anything.

Press the red reset button on the bottom after the unit has cooled for a few minutes. If it trips again right away, the motor is failing or the jam is still there. Do not use a screwdriver, and never reach into the chamber.

Before we arrive: disposal emergency steps

Four steps protect your hands and the cabinet.

Cut the power

Switch off the disposal and the breaker. Unplug a cord-connected unit if the outlet is dry.

Stop the water

Do not run the faucet into a leaking disposal. Close the angle stops if water is dripping from the supply.

Keep hands out

Even a switched-off unit can have a stuck blade. Use tongs only on a dead, unplugged unit.

Clear the cabinet

Take out cleaners and put a bucket under the leak. Tell us the brand on the label.

Repair or replace the disposal

A jam, a tripped reset or a loose drain connection is a repair. A cracked housing, a seized motor or a leak from the shell is a replacement. Under-sink units are commonly sold in ranges from about 1/3 to 1 horsepower, and a larger motor handles scraps more easily.

Many disposals last years, but an old unit rusting through the shell is not worth a second repair. We tell you which side of the line yours is on before work starts. A clogged line past the disposal is a drain cleaning job, not a motor problem.

Installing a new disposal where there was none

Some kitchens have no disposal at all, and the sink drain just runs to the trap. Adding one means a larger sink flange, a mounting ring, a discharge elbow to the trap and, if there is a dishwasher, a connection for its drain hose. A dishwasher knockout plug inside the new unit must be removed, or the dishwasher will not drain.

A disposal also needs power. Some are hardwired and some plug into an outlet under the sink, and what is allowed or required depends on the unit and the local electrical code. The township permit page lists plumbing fixtures as permit work, so call Code Enforcement at (215) 643-3131 to ask how a disposal install is treated. Use a licensed electrician if a new circuit or outlet is needed. We do not run new electrical circuits.

What not to put down a disposal

Grease and oil

They coat the pipe and harden. This is the main reason kitchen lines back up. See hydro jetting for a heavily coated line.

Fibrous scraps

Celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves wrap around the blades.

Pits and bones

Large pits and bones can jam the flywheel.

Rice, pasta and coffee grounds

They swell or pack down in the trap and slow the drain.

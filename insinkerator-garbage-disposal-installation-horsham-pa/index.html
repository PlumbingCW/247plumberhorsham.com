InSinkErator Garbage Disposal Installation in Horsham, PA: Homeowner Guide

An InSinkErator garbage disposal is one of the more doable kitchen upgrades, but the job touches the sink drain, the dishwasher hose and an electrical connection all at once. This guide for Horsham, PA homeowners explains how to pick a model, what the install involves from start to finish, and where a plumber earns the visit.

It also covers what the Horsham Water and Sewer Authority says about what belongs down a drain, because a disposal does not change what your sewer line can handle.

Start with what your sink and cabinet can take

Check three things before you buy: the sink flange opening, the space under the sink and the drain layout. Most kitchen sinks use a standard 3.5 inch drain opening, and InSinkErator disposals mount with a clamp-style assembly that fits it. A shallow cabinet or a deep single bowl can leave less room for the unit, so measure from the flange down to the trap.

Sinks with a second bowl or a dishwasher add one more question. The disposal will connect to the dishwasher drain through a small side inlet, and the hose has to rise above the drain line before it connects. That loop is what keeps dirty sink water out of your dishwasher.

Picking horsepower and feed type

Cabinet fitCompact bodies suit tight cabinetsLarger motors need more depth below the flange

A bigger motor handles fibrous scraps and bone fragments more easily, but it is not a license to put everything down it. A continuous-feed unit is simple, while batch feed adds a safety step that some households prefer around small children. If you cannot decide, a mid-range motor covers most family kitchens.

What goes into the installation, step by step

  1. 1

    Cut power and clear the cabinet

    Turn off the breaker for the kitchen circuit, then confirm the outlet or switch is dead with a tester. Empty the cabinet and lay down a towel or tray.

  2. 2

    Remove the old drain or disposal

    Disconnect the trap, loosen the old mounting ring and lift out the old sink strainer. Scrape off the old plumber's putty before the new flange goes in.

  3. 3

    Mount the flange and the unit

    Set the new flange in fresh putty, secure the mounting assembly from below and hang the disposal on its lugs. Rotate it until the discharge faces the trap arm, then lock it.

  4. 4

    Connect the dishwasher and drain

    If you have a dishwasher, knock out the plug inside the disposal's dishwasher inlet before attaching the hose, or the dishwasher will not drain. Reconnect the trap with new slip joints.

  5. 5

    Wire, test and run

    Connect the power cord or the hardwired leads, restore the breaker and run water for a test. Check the mounting ring, the hose clamp and every slip joint for drips with a dry paper towel.

The one step people forget

Most dishwasher drain connections on a disposal ship with a solid knockout plug in the inlet. If the plug is left in, the dishwasher fills with dirty water after every cycle. Remove it with a hammer and screwdriver, then fish the loose piece out of the disposal chamber.

If the plug has already dropped inside the disposal, it can jam the grind plate, so the unit should never be turned on before it is retrieved.

When a plumber should do the job

No outlet or switch under the sink

A new circuit or switch has to be run to code, and electrical rules belong with a licensed electrician or a plumber working with one.

Corroded or slow sink drain

Installing a disposal on a drain that is already scaled up passes the clog to a new machine. A drain fix comes first.

Mismatched pipe sizes

Older kitchens with odd trap arms or galvanized drain stubs often need new fittings before the disposal can seat.

Leaking from the unit

A leak from the flange, the dishwasher inlet or the body can mean a bad seal or a cracked housing. See our garbage disposal repair page for repair options.

What a disposal should never see

A disposal grinds food into small particles, but it does not make grease, starch or fibers safe for your pipes. Grease cools and coats the drain wall, rice and pasta swell into paste, and celery strings wrap around the grinding parts. Run cold water before, during and for about fifteen seconds after use to carry the waste down the line.

Keep out of the disposal

  • Cooking grease, oil and fatty meat trimmings
  • Coffee grounds in large amounts
  • Pasta, rice and bread that swell in water
  • Potato peels, artichoke and corn husks that mat or string
  • Bones larger than a small chicken bone
  • Egg shells in bulk, onion skins and fruit pits
  • Any non-food object, including bottle caps and utensils

What affects the cost of installing a disposal

What affects the price of a disposal installation

The unit is only part of the invoice. These are the variables a plumber weighs before quoting.

Motor size and model

Higher horsepower and more sound insulation raise the price of the unit itself.

Replacement versus first-time install

Swapping an existing disposal reuses the mounting and wiring. A first-time install may need a new drain flange, a trap rebuild and power.

Electrical needs

Adding an outlet or a switch under the sink is separate work and may need a permit.

Dishwasher and drain condition

A worn drain hose, a missing high loop or a scaled-up drain adds parts and time.

Haul-away and cabinet access

A tight cabinet slows the work, and old units need to be disposed of.

Drains in Horsham: what the local record says

The Horsham Water and Sewer Authority publishes a wipes and fats, oils and grease sheet that names garbage disposals directly: homeowners with a disposal should follow the same grease rules and keep fatty foods out of it. The sheet says grease hardens as it cools and sticks to the walls of sewer pipes, and that hot water does not prevent this. Its advice is to collect grease in a lidded can and wipe pans with a paper towel before washing.

Its rules also state that the customer installs and maintains the building lateral, the pipe from your house to the property line. A kitchen drain that keeps clogging despite a new disposal may point to grease buildup in that pipe, and the sewer camera inspection tells you which part of the line is the problem.

Check with the Horsham Township code enforcement office on whether your job needs a plumbing or electrical permit, because permit rules vary by project. If you are adding a new circuit, ask before you start.

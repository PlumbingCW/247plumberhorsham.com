When patching stops making sense

One repair on an otherwise healthy line is normal. Three repairs in a year, on the same material, usually signal that the pipe has aged out. At that point, each patch costs a visit and a wall opening while the next failure waits somewhere else.

Look for these patterns: pinhole leaks that reappear, low flow at several fixtures, orange or brown water when you open a tap after a few hours, or a house with metal pipe from an older build. Our leak detection work often turns up the evidence that a whole run is failing.

As general industry knowledge, not a Horsham survey, galvanized steel supply was common before about the 1960s and copper took over from the 1950s on, so an older house can hold a mix. We identify the pipe from the basement before you decide.

Copper or PEX for the new lines

Copper

  • Long track record in homes
  • Resists UV and heat
  • Soldered joints take skilled labor
  • Can pinhole in some water conditions

PEX

  • Flexible, fewer fittings and joints
  • Fewer wall openings because it snakes through
  • Not for sunlight exposure or open flame near it
  • Usually quicker to install

Either one is a sound replacement. We pick based on your layout, access and budget, and we tell you what we would choose in your house and why. If you plan to swap fixtures at the same time, choose models that meet those limits so the inspection goes smoothly.

How a repipe actually goes

  1. 1

    Walkthrough and pipe survey

    We trace the supply from the meter through the basement, check fixtures and pressure, and mark where walls will need access.

  2. 2

    Plan the routing

    New lines usually run through basements, crawlspaces and attics to keep wall openings small.

  3. 3

    Install new supply lines

    We run the new main and branch lines, connect fixtures and water heater, and label the shutoffs.

  4. 4

    Test, flush and restore service

    We pressure-test the system, flush the lines and check every fixture. Patching drywall and paint is a separate trade.

Water is off for stretches during the project, and a typical repipe is measured in days, not weeks. The length depends on house size, number of baths and access, and we give a schedule before you agree.

Partial repipe or whole house

Sometimes only one run has failed. A single bathroom group, a kitchen line or a slab-to-wall section can be replaced on its own when the rest tests sound. We can show you what is wrong with each section.

A repipe is also the moment to vet the crew. Pennsylvania does not license most construction contractors at the state level, and the state keeps no plumber list. Home improvement contractors doing $5,000 or more a year must register with the Attorney General, so check any bid at hicsearch.attorneygeneral.gov or call 1-888-520-6680 before you sign.

If the rest is the same age and material as the failed part, though, a partial repipe may only delay the next opening. We will lay out both and the trade-offs, and you decide. If the issue is the line from the street, see water service line repair instead.

What repiping does and does not fix

New supply pipe cures rust-colored water that comes from steel pipe, low flow from scaled lines and the cycle of pinhole leaks. It will not change the pressure or the quality of the water arriving from the street. The Horsham Water and Sewer Authority says lead in tap water comes mainly from service lines and home plumbing, and after any pipe work it suggests running a tap 30 seconds to 2 minutes before drinking.

It also does not touch drains. Slow sinks and tubs are a separate problem handled under drain service, and the supply and drain systems are repaired independently. A repipe is a good time to replace old shutoffs, supply stops and hose bibs that would otherwise fail soon after.

Living in the house during the work

What to expect and arrange

  • Water is shut off for stretches of each day, so fill containers for drinking and flushing
  • Move furniture and valuables away from walls and ceilings where we will open access
  • Plan for dust, since drywall cutting is part of most repipes
  • Tell us about pets, medical equipment or baby needs that depend on running water
  • Keep the path to the basement and main shutoff clear

Signs to schedule a repipe visit

Rusty or discolored water

Brown water that clears after a minute can point to corroding steel pipe.

Low pressure at upstairs fixtures

Narrowed galvanized pipe loses flow where it runs longest.

Pinholes or stains in several rooms

Water marks appearing in different parts of the house suggest wide wear.

Metallic taste or smell

A metallic taste can signal corrosion, though water testing is separate from plumbing.

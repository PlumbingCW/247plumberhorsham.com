What the burner does and how it fails

In a gas tank water heater the burner sits in a sealed compartment under the tank. A pilot or electronic igniter lights it, a thermocouple or flame sensor confirms the flame is burning, and the gas control valve opens and closes to hold the water temperature. The flame heats the bottom of the tank, and exhaust rises through a flue in the center.

Burners fail slowly. Dust, rust flakes from the tank bottom and lint choke the small ports, the flame turns yellow or lazy, and recovery times grow longer. Corrosion on the burner tube or a failing thermocouple can also shut the flame off after a few seconds.

Yellow or flickering flame

A healthy flame is mostly blue. Yellow means poor combustion from dirt, rust or a restricted air supply, and it can produce carbon monoxide.

Pilot keeps going out

Often a weak thermocouple or a draft. If a clean and new thermocouple do not fix it, the gas valve may be failing.

Popping or rumbling

Sediment on the tank floor traps steam under the flame. The burner may be fine, but the tank is working hard.

Soot around the base

Black marks on the cover or draft hood are a warning of incomplete burning. Turn the heater off and have it inspected.

Gas smell? Leave first.

If you smell gas near the water heater, do not light anything, do not flip switches and do not call from inside. Leave the house, then call 911 or your gas utility from outside (PECO customers: 1-800-841-4141).

Only after the utility clears the home should a plumber look at the burner. Never try to relight a pilot when you can smell gas.

What affects the price of a burner replacement

We do not quote a flat price for burner work because these factors change from one water heater to the next. Here is what moves the number.

Which part has actually failed

A cleaned burner or a new thermocouple is a very different job from a new gas control valve or a full burner assembly. The diagnosis comes first, so you pay for the real fault and not a guess.

Brand, model and parts availability

Some manufacturers sell the burner and the gas valve as separate parts, and others sell them as a matched set. Older and discontinued models can mean a hunt for parts or a substitute that needs extra fitting.

Tank age and condition

If the tank bottom is rusted, leaking or full of hardened sediment, putting a new burner under it buys little. The age on the rating plate is part of the quote conversation.

Venting and code condition

A rusted flue, loose draft hood or poor combustion air gets corrected when the burner is serviced, and that adds labor and parts. Safety fixes come before the new part.

Timing and access

After-hours calls and tight closets or attic installs take more time. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

Repair or replace: the decision point

Burner repair usually makes sense when

  • The tank is dry, sound and not very old
  • The fault is a single part such as a thermocouple, igniter or burner tube
  • Parts for your model are in stock
  • The heater held temperature well until this problem began

Replacement usually makes sense when

  • There is any water at the tank base or rust streaks on the seams
  • The heater is near or past the age printed on its rating plate
  • The repair needs a gas valve and a burner on an older unit
  • You want a faster recovery or a different size

Find the manufacture date on the rating plate. Many brands encode the month and year in the serial number, and the format varies by brand, so a photo of the label sent to us is usually enough to read it. A heater with a failed burner and a failing tank should be replaced, not nursed along.

Safety steps while you wait

If your gas water heater is acting up

  • Turn the temperature dial to its lowest setting or the pilot position
  • Close the gas shutoff on the supply pipe to the heater if it is easy to reach
  • Open a window if the room smells stuffy, and keep the area clear of paper and paint
  • Never block the flue or the air openings around the heater
  • Check that your carbon monoxide alarm is on and has fresh batteries
  • If any alarm sounds or you smell gas, leave and call 911 or the gas utility

What the visit looks like

A technician checks gas supply pressure, the flame color, the flue draw and the thermocouple output, then opens the burner compartment. They look for rust, scale and signs of a back-drafting flue. If the burner can be cleaned and adjusted, that comes first.

When a part needs replacing, we tell you the part name, the reason and whether the heater's age and tank condition justify the work. If replacing the whole unit is the more sensible move, our water heater replacement team can talk through size, venting and timing, and our water heater repair team handles the smaller fixes.

Neighboring towns, same approach

We take calls in Warrington and Hatboro as well as Horsham, and the diagnosis steps are the same. A heater that shows a yellow flame or soot is treated as a safety call first and a price call second.

Keep the area around the heater clear, flush a few gallons from the drain valve once a year if the manufacturer's manual says to, and test your carbon monoxide alarm monthly. Small habits like those delay the day the burner clogs and the tank wears out.

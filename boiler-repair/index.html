Gas smell or no heat: what to do first

If you smell gas near the boiler, do not touch switches, do not use a phone inside and do not try to relight anything. Go outside, then call 911 or your gas utility (for PECO customers, 1-800-841-4141), as the Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission advises. We work on the boiler only after the utility has cleared the house.

If there is no gas odor and no carbon monoxide alarm, the situation is urgent but manageable. Check the thermostat, confirm the boiler switch is on and note the pressure gauge reading and any error light. Those details help the dispatcher decide what to bring.

Before we arrive: keep the house safe in a heat outage

A few steps keep pipes from freezing and the house livable while you wait.

Check the basics

Confirm the thermostat is set to heat and the batteries are fresh, the boiler switch and breaker are on, and the fuel supply is open.

Protect the pipes

Open cabinet doors on exterior walls, let a faucet drip on the coldest wall and keep interior doors open so warmer air moves around.

Use safe supplemental heat

Use electric space heaters plugged directly into a wall outlet, away from curtains. Never run a grill, oven or generator indoors, because of the carbon monoxide risk.

Note the symptoms

Write down the pressure reading, any noises, leaks and which rooms are cold. Photos of the gauge and any error code speed up the visit.

Why boilers fail in a Pennsylvania cold snap

A boiler runs hardest when the weather is worst. The longest run times and coldest return water stress circulator pumps, zone valves and the burner, and a part that was marginal in October gives out in January.

Census heating data for 2020 to 2024 shows roughly 1,700 occupied Horsham homes heated with fuel oil, alongside about 4,000 on utility gas. Some of those homes use a boiler and others a furnace, and the Census does not say which. These figures are estimates with margins of error, so we ask what you have rather than assume.

No heat at all

Causes include a tripped switch, a thermostat or power fault, a failed circulator, a lockout on the burner or an empty fuel supply.

Radiators or baseboard cold in some rooms

Trapped air, a stuck zone valve or a closed or clogged section commonly cause part-house heat loss.

Banging, knocking or gurgling

Air in the system, low water or flow problems show up as noise. A loud bang from the boiler itself is worth shutting down.

Water on the floor

Leaks come from valves, pump flanges, the relief valve or a failing boiler section. We find the source before we recommend repair.

Pressure too high or too low

A failing expansion tank or a slow leak shifts system pressure and can open the relief valve.

Boiler keeps shutting off

Short cycling can come from an oversized setting, dirty sensors, a poor vent or a failing limit control.

Hydronic, baseboard and radiator systems: what we repair

A hydronic system heats water in the boiler and pumps it through pipes to baseboard fin tubes or cast iron radiators, then returns it to be heated again. The loops are closed, so air, pressure and flow are the three things to get right.

We repair circulator pumps, zone valves, expansion tanks, air vents and bleeders, pressure relief and fill valves, and the piping around them. We also trace leaks in supply and return lines using the same methods as our leak detection service. Boilers with a separate indirect water heater tank are tested on both sides.

Bleeding air and balancing zones

Air trapped at the top of a radiator or a baseboard run blocks hot water from reaching it. Bleeding the air lets the line fill, and a cold radiator often warms up in minutes. If you bleed it yourself, have a rag and a pan ready, and stop when water, not air, comes out.

If one zone stays cold after bleeding, the problem is probably a zone valve, a thermostat or the circulator. We test each, since replacing the wrong part wastes time and money. Do not keep adding water at the fill valve without finding why pressure dropped, because that points to a leak.

Boiler leaks and water damage

A boiler leak is easy to underestimate because the water often runs along pipes and drains away before you see it. Rust stains on the floor, a crusty buildup on a valve or a gauge that creeps down are all signs of a slow leak.

For an active leak, shut the water feed to the boiler and, if it is safe, turn off its power. If a heating pipe has frozen and split, our burst pipe repair team can handle the pipe while we address the boiler. We do not weld or reseal a cracked boiler section, because that needs replacement of the unit.

What affects the price of boiler repair

Boiler repair prices vary because systems and failures differ so much.

The failed component

An air vent or a thermostat is small. A circulator, a zone valve manifold or a boiler control is more.

System type and age

Older cast iron radiator systems and newer condensing boilers need different parts and testing.

Access to the piping

Boilers in tight utility rooms and lines behind finished walls add labor.

Leak extent

A leaking valve is a small job. A buried or in-wall leak takes detection and opening up.

After-hours timing

A night or weekend call can carry a different call-out charge than a weekday visit, and we state it before you commit.

What we do not do

We do not relight a boiler that has locked out for a safety reason without finding out why. We do not bypass a limit control or a relief valve.

No hot water or water on the floor: when a water heater is an emergency

Cold showers are an annoyance. Water spreading across a finished basement, a pilot that will not stay lit in a closed utility closet, or a tank that hisses and rumbles loudly is a different matter, and those calls get dispatched first.

A tank holds 40 to 50 gallons in most houses, and a failed seam can release a large share of that in under an hour. Water that reaches a furnace, a finished wall or a sump pit does damage that costs far more than the repair itself. So the first job on any leaking-tank call is stopping the water, and the second is finding out where it is coming from.

Not every wet spot is the tank. A loose fitting on the hot or cold line, a dripping relief valve or condensation on a cold pipe can look the same on a concrete floor. We trace it before we recommend anything.

Before we arrive: protect the house and the heater

These steps take a few minutes and limit the damage while a plumber is on the way.

Close the cold supply valve

Turn the handle on the pipe entering the top of the tank a quarter turn until it stops. If the valve is stuck, shut the main at the street side of the meter instead of forcing it.

Cut the power or the gas feed

For an electric unit, switch off its breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to Off. If you smell gas, do none of this: leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility from outside.

Keep people and pets clear

Standing water near a plugged-in heater or a basement outlet can be live. Do not wade through it, and keep children out of the room.

Move what you can

Lift boxes, rugs and stored items off the floor, and photograph the damage for your insurance file before cleanup.

Signs your water heater is failing

Lukewarm water or short hot showers

Often a burnt-out element in an electric tank or a sediment layer insulating the burner on a gas tank. Both are usually fixable.

Popping or rumbling from the tank

Water trapped under hardened sediment boils and bursts upward. The tank may recover after a flush, or it may be near the end of its life.

Rusty or metallic hot water

Rust only on the hot side points at the tank interior or a spent anode rod. Rust on both hot and cold points at the house piping instead.

Water around the base

Could be a fitting, a relief valve or the tank. Leaks from the tank shell itself end in replacement.

Pilot light that keeps going out

Common causes are a worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot tube or a draft problem in the vent. The part is cheap, the diagnosis is what matters.

Breaker that trips when the heater runs

A shorted element or damaged wiring. Do not keep resetting it, because repeated trips can overheat the wiring.

Gas water heater repair: pilot, thermocouple, burner and vent

A gas tank makes heat with a burner under the tank, and a small flame sensor called a thermocouple tells the gas valve the flame is real. When the thermocouple weakens, the valve closes and the heater goes cold. Replacing it is one of the more common gas repairs we see.

The vent matters as much as the burner. A blocked or cooled flue lets combustion gases spill into the room, and a gas heater in a closed basement closet is a carbon monoxide concern. If your detector has sounded, get everyone outside and call 911 before you call anyone else.

If you smell rotten eggs near the heater, leave the building first and call 911 or your gas utility (for PECO customers, 1-800-841-4141) from outside. Do not flip switches or use a phone inside. The Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission gives the same instruction for any gas odor, and we do not work on a gas appliance until the utility has cleared the house.

Electric water heater repair: elements, thermostats and breakers

Most electric tanks carry two heating elements, one high and one low, each with its own thermostat. If you get a little hot water and then nothing, the upper element or its thermostat is the usual suspect. If you get lukewarm water that never gets hot enough, the lower one is.

We test continuity with a meter before replacing anything, because a tripped high-limit reset button and a dead element feel identical from the shower. An element that has sat in sediment for years often fails again soon after, so on an older tank we tell you if the repair is likely to hold.

Hardness in this area is a real factor. The Horsham Water and Sewer Authority lists it at roughly 200 to 350 mg/L as calcium carbonate, about 16 grains per gallon on average. Minerals that heavy settle as scale on elements and tank bottoms, so if your heater is more than a few years old, ask us to check for sediment before you pay for a part.

Tankless water heater problems and error codes

Tankless units rarely fail all at once. They throw an error code, shut down on a flame or temperature fault, or deliver water that runs hot, cold, hot as flow changes. Mineral scale on the heat exchanger and a clogged inlet screen cause many of those faults.

Each brand reads its own codes, so read the display and have the model number ready when you call. Descaling, venting and installation questions are covered on our tankless water heater page.

The practical point: a 1980s house has seen at least two or three tanks, and a basement heater installed on an older gas line or flue may not match today's setup. When we open a utility closet we check the vent and the supply lines too, not only the heater.

Census heating data also shows about 4,000 occupied homes using utility gas and about 3,700 using electricity. Both fuels are common here, so we carry parts for each. A fuel oil household with a separate boiler is a different job, covered under boiler repair.

What affects the price of a water heater repair

Two repairs that sound alike can price very differently. These are the factors that move the number.

The part that failed

A thermocouple or an element is a small part. A gas control valve or a tankless heat exchanger is not.

Access and location

A tank in an open basement is quick to reach. One boxed into a closet or sitting above a finished ceiling takes longer.

Age of the unit

Fittings and valves on an older heater can break when disturbed, and that adds to the work.

Gas, electric or tankless

Each type needs different parts and testing equipment, and tankless parts are usually brand specific.

After-hours timing

A night or weekend dispatch can carry a different call-out charge than a scheduled weekday visit, and we tell you before you commit.

Night and weekend heater failures

Water heaters seem to fail on the coldest Sunday of January. That is partly coincidence and partly load, since a heater working hard in winter exposes a weak element or a tired burner. Our dispatch line answers after hours and on weekends, and we book the earliest same-day or next-morning appointment available.

What we do not do

We do not patch a leaking tank shell, because no repair holds against corrosion from the inside. We do not bypass a relief valve or a safety switch to get hot water faster.

What Wynmere is, and what the public record shows

Wynmere is a named subdivision in Horsham Township, and listings for homes on Wynmere Drive carry the subdivision name, a 19044 ZIP and public water and public sewer. The house types are semi-detached twins and townhouses in a colonial style, and one listing on the street shows an estimated build year of 1984.

That puts Wynmere inside the stretch the township plan describes. Its housing section says over 69 percent of Horsham homes were developed between 1950 and 1990, with another growth surge in the 1980s. Wynmere is a good example of that later wave.

We do not claim to know what is inside your walls from the build year alone. Plumbing in a shared wall home depends on repairs, remodels and past owners, so the first visit is about looking, not assuming.

Living in a twin or townhome changes the plumbing questions

A shared wall means shared sound and sometimes shared risk

A leak from a neighbor's side can show up as a stain on your wall. Note where the water appears and whether the neighbor has seen anything on their side.

Know where your own shutoff is

Many twins have the water entry in the basement or utility closet. Finding the valve and testing that it turns is a five minute job worth doing before you need it.

Compact water heater spaces

Townhome utility closets leave little room. A replacement unit has to fit the space, the vent path and the drain pan setup, so measure before you shop.

Shared lot lines and cleanouts

On narrow lots a cleanout may sit close to the property line. Locate yours before a backup, because finding it during one costs time.

Who owns which sewer and water pipe in Horsham

Horsham Water and Sewer Authority serves about 93 percent of the township. Its published rules split the sewer line at the right-of-way. The Authority is responsible for the service lateral out to that line, and the customer owns and maintains the building lateral from the house to it.

If a drain backs up, call the Authority and a plumber

The Authority will check the street main at no charge when you call its office at (215) 672-8011. If the main is clear, the blockage is on the building lateral, which is yours to clear and repair.

The Authority also notes that most basic homeowner policies do not cover sewer backups. Reading your policy for sewer, drain and sump backup coverage is worth doing before a problem.

The water side works the same way. The Authority maintains the line from the main to the curb stop, and you own the Building Water Service Line from there to the house. A leak on your side is yours to repair, and a quiet meter test can tell you whether you have one.

Water heater replacement and the Horsham permit

Horsham Township lists plumbing fixtures, water heaters included, among the work that needs a permit. Code Enforcement is at 1025 Horsham Road and can be reached at (215) 643-3131. The township says inspections generally need 24 to 48 hours of notice, and it can fine work begun before a permit.

If your tank is leaking at the seams, replacement is the usual answer. If it leaks at a fitting or valve, a repair may be enough. A plumber looking at the tank, the fittings and the drain pan can tell you which. See our notes on water heater replacement and water heater repair.

Before you call, a short checklist

What helps the first conversation

  • Find the main shutoff and confirm it turns.
  • Note the street address and which floor the problem is on.
  • Take a photo of the leak, the stain or the water heater label.
  • Say whether the problem affects one fixture or the whole house.
  • If it is a backup, say which drains gurgle and when.

For drains, see drain cleaning and sewer line cleaning. For a hidden leak, see leak detection. For a failing supply line, see water line repair.

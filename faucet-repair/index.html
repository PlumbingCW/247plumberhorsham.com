Start under the sink: which leak is it?

Dry the cabinet floor with a towel, then run the faucet for a minute. Watch where the first drop forms. Water at the faucet base means a worn cartridge or seals, water at the supply connections means a loose or failing line, and water at the drain tailpiece or trap means a slip nut or washer.

Put a dry paper towel on the cabinet floor overnight. A pea-sized wet spot tells you which part to replace, and tells us what to bring. If the cabinet floor is swollen or smells musty, mention it when you call.

Faucet problems we repair

Drips from the spout

Compression faucets drip from worn washers and seats. Cartridge and ceramic-disc faucets drip when the cartridge wears out.

Leaking tub faucet

A leaky bathtub faucet usually means a worn cartridge or stem behind the handle. The tub spout diverter can also fail.

Low pressure at one faucet

Mineral buildup in the aerator is the common culprit. If every faucet is weak, the problem is elsewhere in the house.

Handle loose or stiff

A stripped set screw or worn cartridge. Newer cartridges restore smooth movement.

No hot water at one tap

Often a clogged cartridge or crossed supply. If it is the whole house, see water heater repair.

Hose bibb leaks

Outdoor spigots leak after a frozen winter and waste water through the wall.

Before we arrive: stop the sink leak

A few minutes of prep keeps the cabinet and floor dry.

Close the angle stops

Turn both small valves under the sink clockwise. Hot and cold are separate.

Empty the cabinet

Pull out cleaners and stored items, then put a bucket under the leak.

Protect cabinet and floor

Wipe up standing water so the particleboard does not swell. Keep power strips and disposals unplugged and dry.

If the stops are stuck

Do not force an old valve. Shut the main water valve and call us.

Sink flooding the cabinet right now: when the shutoff valve will not close

Angle stops, the small valves under a sink, seize with mineral deposits after years of sitting open. Older ones use a multi-turn stem with a rubber washer. Newer quarter-turn valves have a ball inside and last longer.

If the valve won't close or drips from the stem, we replace it. Most are chrome-plated brass with a compression or sweat connection, and the swap takes about as long as the faucet itself. Replace it with the faucet and you won't have to turn off the whole house for the next repair.

Repair or replace the faucet?

Repair makes sense when

  • The faucet is under about ten years old.
  • A cartridge or washer kit is available.
  • The finish is intact and the body is not pitted.
  • Only one handle or the spout leaks.

Replace when

  • The faucet body is corroded or cracked.
  • Parts for the model are no longer sold.
  • Both handles and the spout leak.
  • You want a pull-down sprayer or touchless unit.

EPA WaterSense-labeled bathroom faucets flow at 1.5 gallons per minute or less, which saves water on every wash. A small drip is also a cheap repair, and it stains the basin.

If your faucet job is part of a permitted alteration, Horsham Township Ordinance 3007 (1991) sets the faucet ceiling at 3.0 gallons per minute. A 1.5 gpm WaterSense faucet is half of that, so replacing a worn model with a labeled one clears the rule and trims the water bill.

Supply lines, sink drains and the plumbing under the cabinet

Braided stainless supply lines carry water from the angle stop to the faucet. They are usually 3/8 inch and come in lengths from 9 to 36 inches. They last years, but a bulged braid or a rust spot at a nut means it should be replaced before it bursts.

The drain side is separate. A leaking P-trap, a loose slip nut or a cracked tailpiece drips only when the sink drains. If the sink drains slowly instead, a clog is the issue, and drain cleaning clears it. If the leak seems to come from a wall, we may recommend leak detection first.

What affects the price of faucet and sink repair

A washer swap and a full new faucet with new stops are far apart.

Parts or new fixture

A cartridge is a modest part. A designer faucet can cost many times that.

Valve condition

Replacing seized angle stops adds parts and time.

Sink and countertop type

Undermount or stone countertops make access harder than a drop-in sink.

Supply line and drain parts

Old traps and corroded nuts may need replacing in the same visit.

Time of call

When a business plumbing problem cannot wait until morning

Some failures cost money by the hour. A restaurant without a working three-compartment sink or restroom may have to close for the shift, and a retail unit with sewage on the floor cannot keep customers inside. A warehouse with a split supply line can soak pallets of paper or cardboard goods before anyone notices.

We treat these as emergencies when water is spreading, sewage is backing up, there is no running water to a restroom that serves the public, or a hot water failure stops a kitchen. Call at night or on a weekend and you reach the same 24/7 line. A clear price comes before work starts, and the work order can be written for the tenant or the property manager.

What to do in the first ten minutes

  • Find the fixture shutoff first, then the building main if water keeps flowing. Tag it so the next shift knows.
  • Keep people out of any room with standing water near outlets, panels or floor-mounted equipment.
  • Photograph the damage and the source before anything is moved, for your landlord or insurer.
  • Stop flushing and running sinks if a floor drain or toilet is backing up, since every flush adds to the overflow. Then call HWSA at (215) 672-8011, because it checks the street sewer main free on a blockage call and that shows whether the stoppage is yours.

Where commercial plumbing differs from a house

Commercial buildings run more fixtures on larger pipe, often with several tenants on one main. A restroom group may use flushometer valves that need higher pressure than a home toilet, and a single clogged branch can idle half a floor. Mop sinks, floor drains, hose bibbs and rooftop units add connection points that a house never has.

Restrooms and flushometers

Weak flush, ghost flushing or a valve that will not reseat usually points to a worn diaphragm kit, not a new fixture.

Floor drains and sumps

A drain that gurgles in a back room can be a clogged branch or a failed lift pump. HWSA rules ban storm, roof and sump water from the sanitary sewer, so we flag any such tie-in. See drain cleaning for how we clear the line.

Commercial water heaters

Larger tanks and recirculation loops fail differently from a 40-gallon home tank. For a smaller unit, water heater repair covers the tank-side diagnosis.

Supply and shutoff valves

Old gate valves on a building main often seize or leak when turned. We test them before an emergency, not during one.

Restaurant plumbing and grease trap service

Kitchens send fats, oils and grease down the drain, and these harden inside pipe and interceptors. A grease trap or interceptor that is overdue for pumping will push greasy water back into the kitchen or slow every sink on the line. If you ask us about a pump-out, we will tell you honestly which part we handle and which part a licensed hauler does.

Horsham Water and Sewer Authority rules require grease, oil and sand interceptors at all commercial and industrial establishments, and the owner maintains them. Chemical grease dispersants need Authority approval, so do not pour them in to skip a pump-out. Keep the pump-out receipts. For lines already slowing down, hydro jetting scours grease from the pipe wall rather than punching a hole through it.

Backflow testing for commercial water service

A backflow preventer keeps water that has been in your building, such as irrigation, boiler or process water, from flowing backward into the public supply. Fire sprinkler feeds, irrigation systems and some kitchen equipment commonly require a testable assembly. The test is a short pressure check on the assembly.

If HWSA supplies your building, its rules make customers follow the Authority's Cross Connection Control and Backflow Prevention Program Policy. Ask HWSA at (215) 672-8011 for that policy and the report process before the due date. We repair or replace a failed assembly, and having that policy in hand keeps the paperwork right the first time.

Not every call is a flood. Landlords and property managers call us for recurring slow drains, leaking tenant restrooms, new sink or fixture runs, and tie-ins when a space is rebuilt. Send the work order details and the unit number when you call, and say who approves spending.

What affects the price of commercial plumbing work

A restaurant drain and a one-room office leak are very different jobs. These are the things that move the number.

Access and building type

A ground-floor slab drain is simpler than a line above a drop ceiling over a sales floor.

Pipe size and material

Larger cast iron or copper takes more labor, fittings and cutting time than residential-size pipe.

After-hours timing

Night and weekend calls can carry a different rate than a scheduled weekday visit.

Parts and equipment

Flushometer kits, valves, pumps and backflow assemblies vary widely by size and brand.

Scope and permits

A repair, a replacement and a new run each carry different inspection and permit needs.
